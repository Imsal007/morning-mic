How to speak slower and more clearly

The short answer. To speak more clearly, slow down slightly, finish your word endings, stress the words that carry the meaning and pause between ideas. A comfortable pace for presenting is around 130 to 160 words a minute.

Pace: calm, not slow

Nerves make most people speed up. Aim for roughly 130 to 160 words a minute. That sounds calm without sounding slow, and it gives listeners time to follow you. If you want to check your pace, time yourself reading a 150-word paragraph aloud.

Breath and posture

Stand or sit tall, breathe low into your belly and let the breath out slowly as you speak. A steady breath gives your voice a steady start.

Finish your word endings

Clear endings make you sound precise. Finish every t, d and k at the end of a word. Practise with phrases like "fast and kind" and "first test" until the endings are audible without being forced.

Stress the words that matter

English stresses the words that carry meaning and squeezes the small words between them. In "We cut onboarding from THREE WEEKS to FOUR DAYS", the numbers do the work. Mark the key words in your own sentences and lean on those.

End statements going down

When a statement rises at the end, it sounds like a question and asks for permission. Let your voice fall at the end of a statement. Save the rising tone for real questions.

If English is your second language

The aim is to be understood the first time, not to remove your accent. Pace, word endings and stress on key words usually help more than changing individual sounds. Record yourself, listen for the points where someone might ask you to repeat, and work on those.

Try this now (five minutes)

  1. Read the same 150-word paragraph three times: fast, normal, then slow with clear endings.
  2. Record all three.
  3. Listen and pick the one you would most like to hear from a stranger.
  4. Use that pace on your next real call.

Questions people ask

What is a good speaking pace?

Around 130 to 160 words a minute works well for presentations and pitches. Conversation can be a little faster.

Should I try to lose my accent?

No. Aim to be easy to understand. Clear stress, finished word endings and a steady pace matter more than the accent itself.

Why do people ask me to repeat myself?

Usually the pace is too fast, word endings are dropped or the key words are not stressed. A recording will show you which.
