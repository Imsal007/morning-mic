How to practise an investor pitch alone

The short answer. Practise a pitch by saying it out loud, in short sections, and listening back to a recording. Reading your slides silently does not count as rehearsal. Make the opening and the ask automatic first, then run the whole pitch against a timer.

Why silent practice does not work

In your head the pitch sounds fluent, because you skip the hard parts. Out loud, the gaps show: the sentence that will not finish, the number you forget, the slide that takes three minutes. You only find these by speaking.

Split the pitch into five parts

  1. Hook: the first sentence that earns attention.
  2. Problem: who has it and what it costs them.
  3. Solution and proof: what you built and one real result.
  4. The ask: what you want from this listener.
  5. Close: the line you want them to remember.

Practise each part on its own before you join them.

Record every part and listen for three things

  • Pace: are you rushing the important lines?
  • Fillers: count them, and replace them with pauses.
  • The point: could a stranger repeat your main message after hearing it once?

Practise the opening and the ask the most

The first ten seconds set the tone and the ask is the reason you are in the room. Practise those two until you can say them without thinking, even when you are nervous.

Add the timer

Run the full pitch at the length you will really have. If you have ten minutes, practise for nine so you are not rushing at the end. Do three runs in a row inside the time limit before you call it ready.

Rehearse the questions too

The pitch is only half of the meeting. Practise your answers to the questions you dread, out loud and recorded. Our guide on answering hard investor questions gives a simple structure for it.

Try this now (ten minutes)

  1. Pick the hook and the ask only.
  2. Say both aloud, then record them.
  3. Listen back and fix one thing: pace, a filler, or an unclear sentence.
  4. Record again. Keep the better version and note what changed.

Questions people ask

How many times should I rehearse my pitch?

There is no magic number. Rehearse until the opening and the ask are automatic and you finish inside the time limit three runs in a row.

Should I memorise my pitch word for word?

Memorise the opening and the ask, and know the order of the middle. Word-for-word memory often sounds stiff and falls apart if you lose your place.

How do I practise a pitch alone?

Record yourself on your phone, one section at a time, and listen back. A private recording gives you honest feedback without needing an audience.
