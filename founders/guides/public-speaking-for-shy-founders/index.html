Public speaking for shy founders: start where nobody can hear you

The short answer. If you are shy, start by speaking where nobody can hear you: alone, into a recording. Then build up in small steps, so each one feels only slightly harder than the last, until the real thing is simply the next step.

Shy is not the same as bad at speaking

Many shy founders are careful, prepared and clear thinkers. What gets in the way is the fear of being watched. That is a different problem from skill, and it is easier to solve in stages.

A ladder, one small step at a time

  1. Read a paragraph aloud, alone.
  2. Answer a question aloud, alone, and record it.
  3. Listen back to yourself.
  4. Play a recording to one person you trust.
  5. Speak to one person live, then a small group or a call.
  6. Speak on camera, at a meeting or on a stage.

The first three steps are private. Most people skip them and start at step five, which is why it feels so hard.

Prepare more, not harder

Confidence often comes from knowing what you are going to say. Prepare your opening line and your main point for anything you have to deliver, and rehearse them out loud. You do not need more courage if the first thirty seconds are already solid.

Start with what you know best

Practise on your own company, your own customers and your own story. You are the expert on those, so the only thing left to practise is delivery.

Use your breath

Nerves speed you up. Before you start, breathe in through your nose and out slowly, and begin the first sentence at a calm pace. A steady start makes the rest easier to control.

Try this now (five minutes)

  1. Alone, answer aloud: "Why did you start your company?" for 45 seconds.
  2. Record it and listen once, without judging.
  3. Note one thing that worked and one thing to change.
  4. Do it again tomorrow with that one change.

Questions people ask

Can introverts be good at public speaking?

Yes. Careful preparation and clear thinking are real strengths. Speaking is a skill you can practise, whatever your personality.

Should I join a speaking group?

Groups work well for some people. If the idea of a room is the obstacle, start privately first and add a group later.

How do I stop my voice shaking?

Slow your first sentence, breathe out fully before you start, and know your opening line by heart. Nerves usually settle once you are a few sentences in.
