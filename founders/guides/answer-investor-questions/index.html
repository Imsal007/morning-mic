How to answer hard investor questions without waffling

The short answer. Answer a hard question in three moves: agree with the concern, ask a short question to find out what they are really worried about, then answer in under 30 seconds. Never argue with an objection, get curious about it.

Why we waffle

A tough question triggers a defence reflex, and we talk more to feel safer. The result is a long answer that buries the point. A short, structured answer sounds more confident than a long one.

Agree, ask, answer

  1. Agree: "That's a fair concern." It lowers the temperature and buys you a second.
  2. Ask: "Is it the size of the market or how we reach it that worries you?" Now you answer the real question.
  3. Answer: keep it under 30 seconds.

Keep the answer short with PREP

Structure the answer as Point, Reason, Example, Point. State your answer first, give one reason, show one example and finish by repeating the point. It stops you wandering and gives you a clear place to stop.

Questions worth practising

  • Who are your competitors, and why do you win?
  • Why now?
  • Why are you the right team?
  • How big can this get?
  • What happens if a bigger company copies you?
  • What is your biggest risk?

When you do not know

Say so plainly, then say how you would find out: "I don't have that number yet. Here is how we are measuring it and when we will have it." Honest and specific is stronger than a guess.

Try this now (ten minutes)

  1. Pick the question you dread most.
  2. Record your answer using agree, ask, answer.
  3. Check that the answer part is under 30 seconds.
  4. Record it again with a clearer first sentence.

Questions people ask

How long should an answer to an investor question be?

Aim for under 30 seconds for most questions. If they want more, they will ask.

What if the question is hostile?

Stay calm, agree with whatever is fair and ask what is behind the question. Treating it as a real question usually changes the tone.

How do I practise Q&A alone?

Write down the ten hardest questions, record an answer to each one and listen back for length and clarity.
