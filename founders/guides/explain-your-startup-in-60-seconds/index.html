How to explain your startup in 60 seconds

The short answer. Explain your startup in 60 seconds with five parts: who it is for, the problem, what you do, one piece of proof and what you want next. That is roughly 150 words, which takes about a minute at a normal speaking pace.

The five parts

  1. Who it is for: name the person, not the market.
  2. The problem: what is painful or expensive for them today.
  3. What you do: one plain sentence, no jargon.
  4. Proof: one real result, number or customer.
  5. The ask: what you want from this listener.

A fill-in template

We help [who] [do what] so they can [outcome]. Today, [problem]. We [how]. [proof]. [the ask].

An example

This is an invented example to show the shape:

We help growing companies get new starters productive in their first week. Today onboarding takes weeks, and managers lose days answering the same questions. We turn your existing documents into a guided first-week plan. One team cut onboarding from three weeks to four days. I'd like to show you how it would work for your hiring plan.

How to tighten it

  • Lead with the outcome, not the technology.
  • Cut any word your customer would not use.
  • Say it out loud, then cut another 20%.
  • Try it on someone outside your industry. If they can repeat it back, it works.

Try this now (ten minutes)

  1. Fill in the template in writing.
  2. Say it aloud with a 60-second timer running.
  3. Record it, listen back and mark any sentence that sounded unclear.
  4. Rewrite those sentences shorter and record again.

Questions people ask

How long should an elevator pitch be?

Between 30 and 60 seconds. Have a 30-second version ready as well, because you will not always be given a full minute.

Should I explain the technology?

Only if your listener cares about it. Most buyers and investors want the problem, the result and why you are credible.

What if my product is complicated?

Explain the outcome for the customer first. You can go deeper when they ask, and their questions show you where to go.
