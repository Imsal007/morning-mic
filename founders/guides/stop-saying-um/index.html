How to stop saying um and other filler words

The short answer. You stop saying um by replacing it with a silent pause. Fillers are mostly your brain buying time, so the fix is to get comfortable with a second of silence and to know your first sentence before you open your mouth.

Why you say um

When you speak, you are thinking and talking at the same time. A filler fills the gap while the next idea arrives. To you the silence feels long. To a listener, a short pause sounds like someone who is choosing their words.

Common fillers are um, uh, like, you know, kind of, sort of and basically. Words like "basically" and "so" at the start of a sentence count too.

Step 1: Count your fillers

Record yourself for 60 seconds answering "What does your company do?" Play it back and count every filler. Write the number down. This is your baseline, and it is the number you will beat.

Step 2: Replace each one with a pause

Play the recording again and, at every filler, stop and breathe in through your nose. Aim for a pause of about half a second to a second. Then record the same answer again, pausing where the fillers used to be.

Step 3: Know your first sentence

Fillers cluster at the start of an answer and at the points where you switch ideas. Prepare a one-line opener for the questions you get most: what you do, why you started, who it is for. When the first sentence is ready, the rest follows more easily.

Step 4: Slow down a little

Fast speakers often use fillers because they outrun their own thinking. A comfortable range for presenting is around 130 to 160 words a minute. Slowing down slightly gives your brain time to find the next word without a sound in the gap.

Step 5: Repeat the same answer

Record the same answer on several days and compare the counts. Practising one answer repeatedly lets you hear your own progress, which is more useful than a general feeling that you are improving.

Try this now (five minutes)

  1. Set a timer for 60 seconds and answer: "What does your company do?"
  2. Play it back and count the fillers.
  3. Answer again, and at every point where you would say a filler, close your mouth and breathe in.
  4. Count again. Write both numbers down.

Questions people ask

Is it bad to say um?

A few fillers are normal in conversation. In a pitch, talk or video, too many make you sound unsure, so the goal is fewer, not zero.

How long does it take to stop saying um?

It varies from person to person, so measure your own count on the same answer rather than trusting a timeline.

Why do I say "basically" and "like" so much?

They are habits that fill space while you think. The same fix works for all of them: notice them on a recording, then replace them with a pause.
